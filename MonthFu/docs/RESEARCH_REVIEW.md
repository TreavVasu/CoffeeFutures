# Research review for MonthFu

Reviewed 2026-09-30. This note records the reasoning behind the monthly experiment, the earlier results that remain usable as context, and the boundaries of any improvement claim. Measured MonthFu results belong in the generated reports and metrics; this review does not substitute the old five-day scores for monthly validation.

## Repository evidence reviewed

The project documentation reviewed was `README.md`, `Approach.md`, `Summary.MD`, `Limitations.md`, `data/COT/COFFEE_C_COT_COLUMN_GUIDE.md`, `Scripts/project/docs/Features.md`, `Scripts/project/docs/MODELING.md`, `Scripts/project/docs/MODELING_30D.md`, `Scripts/project/docs/NEWS_EVENT_TRANSFORMATION.md`, and `Scripts/project/artifacts/outputs/final_news_integrated_model_report.md`. Third-party package readmes inside `.venv` are dependency documentation and do not describe this research. The supplied PDF papers were extracted locally, and their objectives, methods, evaluation and reported findings were examined. The GARCH table discussed below was also checked against its rendered PDF page.

The old `research_ensemble` achieved a reported five-session RMSE of 5.3443% and direction accuracy of 50.15%. The corrected matched news experiment did not improve RMSE or direction accuracy. The documentation explicitly withdraws the earlier 61.35% direction result because future-price information entered the upstream news selection/scoring. None of those five-day metrics is a like-for-like benchmark for a monthly target.

An existing thirty-day implementation was also present. Its default was 30 observed trading sessions, approximately six weeks. It already provided useful publication-aware COT features, weather delays, train-only preprocessing, purging by target-end dates, and quarterly evaluation. MonthFu builds on those ideas while making one-month horizons explicit and extending feature engineering and bounded optimization.

## Horizon choice and COT cadence

The primary target should represent 30 calendar days: `Close[first observed session on/after origin + 30 calendar days] / Close[origin] - 1`. No weekend price is interpolated. An absent price observation can extend the target date, and `target_end_date` records the actual observed close used. The final origins without a mature target remain inference rows.

Useful alternatives have distinct meanings:

| Horizon | Meaning | COT relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 calendar days | Fixed approximately one-month business question | Normally spans around four reporting weeks, without fixing a specific number of price sessions. |
| 28 calendar days | Four complete weeks | Convenient weekly-cycle comparison; release holidays/delays still need explicit handling. |
| 21 calendar days | Three complete weeks | Shorter supply/positioning response comparison; not equivalent to 21 market sessions. |
| 21 observed sessions | Approximately a trading month | A conventional market-session horizon that varies in calendar duration. |
| 30 observed sessions | Approximately six weeks | Retained as an explicit optional extension; should never silently mean one month. |

A horizon cannot be chosen merely because its raw RMSE is smaller: a shorter target typically has a smaller return variance. Compare each model against its own matched zero-return and previous-approach baseline, with normalized development skill `1 - MSE(model) / MSE(zero)`. Make horizon choice on pre-holdout validation, then freeze it before comparing holdout outcomes. Report all horizon results without selecting a second winner from holdout scores.

COT is weekly positioning, not physical coffee delivery. The [CFTC description and FAQ](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm) state that positions generally describe Tuesday and are released Friday at 15:30 US Eastern; holidays can alter that schedule. Publication is therefore the availability anchor, not the report's Tuesday snapshot date. The implementation conservatively starts use on the first observed market session strictly after publication day, generally Monday after a normal Friday release.

Weekly changes, 4-report changes, acceleration, positioning z-scores and positioning indices should be calculated on report rows before carrying them onto daily rows. Daily repetition of a weekly value must not turn a four-week change into a four-session change. Backward as-of alignment carries only the latest available value. Do not backward-fill pre-release days, interpolate toward the next weekly report, or forward-fill information from an unavailable source. Report/release ages and stale flags make the carry-forward auditable.

The CFTC FAQ also states that it does not provide a complete historical release-date list. Ordinary historical publication dates remain estimates unless verified by overrides or announcements; special delays and conservative guards improve alignment but do not make every historical timestamp certified. Keep snapshot, publication, feature-availability and first usable dates separate in the audit.

The [ICE Coffee C specifications](https://www.ice.com/products/15/Coffee-C-Futures/specs) describe a physical futures contract. This is separate from COT publication cadence. Continuous Yahoo closes also do not supply a full dated-contract delivery/roll calendar or a term structure, so the monthly model must not claim to predict an executable specific-contract trade.

## Priorities for feature engineering

1. Prefer stable, scale-aware price inputs: trailing 21/28/30/63/126-session momentum, mean-relative bias, drawdown, downside risk, volatility ratios and trend efficiency. Add modest interactions such as trend times volume or volatility regime. Preserve source-quality flags and avoid interpreting anomalous tiny volume as a real flow signal.
2. Keep COT normalized by open interest. Explore net/gross positioning, weekly changes over 1/4/8/13 reports, acceleration, 26/52-report z-scores, crowding and concentration. Positioning times price momentum can represent continuation versus crowded reversal without asserting causality.
3. Use trailing 7/30/60/90-day weather and seasonal anomalies computed from previous years only. Brazil flowering/harvest/winter interactions are fixed hypotheses; do not use a full-year climatology containing future data. Retrospective reanalysis and incomplete regional coverage remain limits even after a conservative availability lag.
4. Give a compact economically chosen group a fair chance against the large feature bank. Use regularization, minimum leaves, bounded train windows, time decay, volatility normalization and shrinkage to zero as development-only choices. More features increase selection risk as well as expressive power.
5. Exclude the supplied retrospective news universe from the primary selection. Counts and fixed text counts inherit upstream return-based retention and retrospective normalization. A six-session lag covers a five-session outcome-maturity dependency but cannot undo full-history event selection. Optional news results should remain explicitly experimental.

## Validation and improvement claims

- Construct the monthly target and its real endpoint before splitting. Training labels must end strictly before the first validation/refit origin; validation labels must end before the final holdout boundary. A fixed row purge is inadequate for calendar horizons, market holidays or gaps.
- Fit feature availability/variance screening, imputation, indicators, scaling and any supervised selection on each training fold only. Disable random internal validation splitting when early stopping is used, or supply a chronological evaluation window with target purging.
- Select algorithms, feature groups, train windows, ensemble weights and shrinkage exclusively from chronological development predictions. Save the selected recipe before holdout evaluation. Quarterly refits can use earlier holdout labels only after maturity; this is a walk-forward holdout, not a single frozen fit.
- Use identical target origins, target definitions and refit dates for selected forecasts and all baselines. Save every refit cutoff and latest training target endpoint.
- The most useful previous-approach reference adapts the original forest, autoregression and causal Holt-Winters components to the same monthly horizon. Exact stored five-day mixture weights are RF 0.4640262047149529, Holt-Winters 0.41934745545223984 and AR 0.11662633983280726. Transferring those weights defines a frozen reference; it is not monthly weight optimization. A separate CV-selected mixture of those components gives a second reference.
- Baseline adaptations must be labeled honestly. The original forest used `technical_ohlcv`, while Ridge(alpha=2) used `price_history`; raw-price inputs, missingness handling, feature coverage, Holt-Winters fit history and forecast-step approximation can differ in MonthFu. A compact proxy or adapted transfer must not be called exact reproduction of the saved model. Always report the zero and expanding historical-mean forecasts as well.
- A zero forecast is neutral; its direction accuracy is not an always-up/down benchmark. Use a direction benchmark whose majority sign is determined from mature training labels. Report RMSE, MAE, relative squared-error skill, correlation and direction together.
- Adjacent monthly targets overlap heavily. Report moving-block comparisons with blocks longer than the horizon and block-length sensitivity, plus real-date disjoint-label samples. Disjoint sample variants themselves share data across offsets, so they are sensitivity diagnostics, not independent replications.
- A negative selected-minus-baseline RMSE difference favors the selected model. If its block interval spans zero, say the improvement is uncertain. A positive point improvement is a historical measurement, not a significance or live-profit guarantee.
- Historical holdout reuse in previous project experiments makes this exploratory research even if the new code never fits current test labels. Quarterly/year-specific results reveal whether a pooled gain comes from one regime. A prospective frozen recipe is required to establish fresh evidence.
- Empirical residual bands estimated on the same CV used for selection can be optimistic. Label them as empirical error bands; evaluate actual holdout coverage and do not promise nominal coverage in future regimes.

## Verification scope

`MonthFu/tests/test_evaluation.py` checks calendar/session target differences, immature tails, invalid calendars, strict maturity at split boundaries, irregular-calendar fold purging, recent windows, disjoint target windows, prefix invariance of price features, training-only preprocessing, neutral zero forecasts, scale-invariant baseline skill, paired block comparisons, and causal/nonmutating Holt-Winters prediction. Source-specific release and weather/news tests are maintained separately. These checks establish timing and reproducibility behavior; out-of-sample forecasts and saved paired metrics establish measured performance.
