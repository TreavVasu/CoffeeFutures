# 03 — Early models, research strategy and leakage review

The supplied project originally predicted the five-observed-session simple return, `Close[t+5] / Close[t] - 1`. Return RMSE was the principal objective; MAE and predicted-sign accuracy were complementary diagnostics. Monthly targets are a different problem, so five-day RMSE cannot demonstrate a monthly improvement.

The earliest [train_arabica_model.py](../../Scripts/project/scripts/train_arabica_model.py) tested regression and classification candidates, including Ridge/logistic models, Extra Trees, random forests, histogram boosting and optional XGBoost. It selected on validation and refitted through validation before scoring the holdout. Its basic 70/15/15 chronological split was **unpurged**: adjacent forward-return labels could cross partition boundaries.

Subsequent top-three COT/weather and feature-combination experiments added weather stress, feature aggregation and ensembles. Two historical issues limit their conclusions. [select_top3_cot_features](../../Scripts/project/scripts/train_top3_cot_weather_model.py) ranks correlations against the full target history before splitting. [experiment_feature_combinations.py](../../Scripts/project/scripts/experiment_feature_combinations.py) chooses models within a feature set on validation, but its overall feature-set winner uses holdout RMSE, with holdout direction as a tie-break. These are implemented exploratory experiments, not untouched-test selection procedures.

[train_research_paper_ensemble.py](../../Scripts/project/scripts/train_research_paper_ensemble.py) tested seventeen candidates, including AR Ridge, ARIMA(1,1,1), exponential smoothing, five-session Holt–Winters, rolling linear/quadratic trends, technical linear/forest models, MLP, ELM, weather/COT Ridge, threshold AR, COT stress forests, histogram boosting and XGBoost variants. Stored metrics confirm XGBoost was enabled. GARCH was evaluated separately against future realized volatility, since volatility modeling does not itself predict return direction.

The script purged five rows at chronological boundaries and optimized nonnegative ensemble weights using validation only. The saved ensemble comprised **46.40% technical random forest, 41.93% Holt–Winters and 11.66% AR Ridge**. On 1,003 holdout origins it achieved **5.3443% RMSE and 50.15% direction accuracy**. The zero-return benchmark was slightly better at **5.3330% RMSE**. ARIMA's 5.3303% RMSE and ELM's 51.74% direction were post-hoc holdout leaders; they did not determine that validation-selected recipe. See the [saved comparison](../../Scripts/project/artifacts/outputs/research_paper_model_comparison.csv).

The papers supplied hypotheses rather than exact replications: price-level studies do not establish return predictability, and spot prices, FX, individual delivery/default records and counterparties were unavailable. Positioning or contract-stress proxies remain labeled as proxies. [RESEARCH_REVIEW.md](RESEARCH_REVIEW.md) documents the evidence.

MonthFu strengthens evaluation with actual target-end-date purging, training-only transformations, expanding validation, frozen recipes, quarterly mature-label refits and overlapping-return uncertainty checks. It adapts the old components to the same monthly target for comparison; it does not reuse old five-day scores as a baseline. Historical holdout reuse still makes results exploratory.
