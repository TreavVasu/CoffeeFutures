# Coffee C COT Column Guide

This guide explains the columns in the filtered `COT/` files created from CFTC Commitments of Traders data for `COFFEE C`. In market terms, Coffee C is the ICE Futures U.S. Arabica coffee benchmark contract. ICE describes Coffee C as the world benchmark for Arabica coffee. One futures contract represents 37,500 pounds of exchange-grade green coffee.

The main file is `COT/coffee_c_all_cot_data.csv`. It combines rows from Legacy Futures Only and Disaggregated Futures Only COT reports, so some columns are blank for some rows. That is normal: Legacy reports use `Commercial` and `Noncommercial`; Disaggregated reports use `Producer/Merchant`, `Swap Dealer`, `Managed Money`, and `Other Reportables`.

## Quick Reading Guide

- A `Long` position is the buyer side of Coffee C futures. Longs generally gain when Coffee C futures prices rise, though commercial longs may simply be hedging future coffee purchases.
- A `Short` position is the seller side. Shorts generally gain when Coffee C futures prices fall, though commercial shorts may be hedging production, inventory, or forward sales.
- Every open Coffee C futures contract has both a long and a short side. COT does not identify the exact buyer matched with the exact seller. It reports aggregate positioning by trader group.
- `Open Interest` is the number of outstanding Coffee C contracts not yet closed, delivered, exercised, or otherwise offset. For Coffee C, multiply contracts by 37,500 pounds to estimate notional pounds of coffee exposure.
- `Change_in_...` means the change versus the prior weekly COT report.
- `Pct_of_OI_...` means the group position as a percent of total Coffee C open interest.
- `Traders_...` means the count of reportable traders in that bucket. Counts can overlap because one trader can have both long and short activity, or spreading activity.
- `All` means all Coffee C contract months combined. `Old` and `Other` are CFTC crop-year buckets used in long-format agricultural reports; for most first-pass analysis use `All`.
- `Spread` means the same trader holds offsetting long and short Coffee C positions, usually across contract months. It is not the same as being outright bullish or bearish.

## Who Is Buying Or Selling Coffee C?

| Group | Appears In | Plain meaning for Arabica coffee | Long side can mean | Short side can mean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Commercial` / `Comm` | Legacy | Large traders using Coffee C mainly to hedge physical coffee business exposure. | Roasters, importers, users, or merchants hedging future purchase costs. | Producers, exporters, merchants, or inventory holders hedging sale price risk. |
| `Noncommercial` / `NonComm` | Legacy | Large traders not classified as commercial hedgers. Often speculative or investment-driven. | Bullish exposure to Coffee C futures. | Bearish exposure or short strategy in Coffee C futures. |
| `Producer/Merchant/Processor/User` / `Prod_Merc` | Disaggregated | Physical coffee supply-chain businesses using futures to manage price risk. | Often coffee users, roasters, importers, or merchants protecting against higher prices, but can include other physical firms. | Often growers, exporters, merchants, or inventory holders protecting against lower prices. |
| `Swap Dealer` / `Swap` | Disaggregated | Dealers hedging commodity swap exposure with Coffee C futures. | Hedge of client swap exposure that leaves dealer needing long futures. | Hedge of client swap exposure that leaves dealer needing short futures. |
| `Managed Money` / `M_Money` | Disaggregated | Funds, CTAs, CPOs, and similar money managers. | Fund positioning that is more bullish or trend-following long. | Fund positioning that is more bearish or trend-following short. |
| `Other Reportables` / `Other_Rept` | Disaggregated | Other large reportable traders not classified above. | Large trader long exposure not in the named categories. | Large trader short exposure not in the named categories. |
| `Nonreportable` / `NonRept` | Both | Smaller traders below CFTC reporting thresholds, derived by subtraction. | Unknown smaller-trader long exposure. | Unknown smaller-trader short exposure. |

Important caveat: CFTC classifies the trader, not each individual trade. A commercial trader can sometimes have speculative-looking positions, and a fund can spread or hedge. Treat COT as positioning pressure, not as a list of physical coffee purchases.

## How This Relates To Arabica Coffee Futures

Coffee C futures are the central listed futures contract for Arabica coffee price risk. The rows in your `COT/` files show how many Coffee C contracts different trader classes held each week. This helps answer questions like: Are funds building a large long? Are commercial hedgers heavily short? Is open interest expanding while price rises? Are positions concentrated in a few large traders?

For modeling, common derived features are: net managed money (`M_Money_Positions_Long_ALL - M_Money_Positions_Short_ALL`), net noncommercial, net commercial, percent-of-open-interest versions of those, weekly changes, and concentration ratios.

## Report Types In These Files

| Local dataset | CFTC style | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `legacy_futures_only` | Legacy Futures Only | Long continuous history. Uses broad `Commercial`, `Noncommercial`, and `Nonreportable` groups. |
| `disaggregated_futures_only` | Disaggregated Futures Only | Better category detail from 2006/2009 onward. Separates physical businesses, swap dealers, managed money, and other reportables. |

## Reusable Column Grammar

Most columns are built from these pieces:

- Measure: `Positions`, `Change_in`, `Pct_of_OI`, `Traders`, `Conc`, or `Open_Interest`.
- Trader group: `Comm`, `NonComm`, `Prod_Merc`, `Swap`, `M_Money`, `Other_Rept`, `Tot_Rept`, or `NonRept`.
- Side: `Long`, `Short`, or `Spread`.
- Bucket: `All`, `Old`, or `Other`.

Example: `Pct_of_OI_M_Money_Long_All` means the percent of all Coffee C open interest held long by Managed Money traders.

## Notes On Historical Spelling Variants

Some historical CFTC files contain typos or abbreviations in column names, such as `Postions`, `Spead`, or `Othr`. These should be read as `Positions`, `Spread`, and `Other`. The notebook keeps the original column names so the filtered files remain traceable to the CFTC source files.
